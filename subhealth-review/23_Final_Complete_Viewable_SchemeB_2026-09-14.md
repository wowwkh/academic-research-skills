## English Abstract (298 words, structured, no citations)

**Background**: Suboptimal health status affects 60-70% of urban professionals, operationally defined by Sub-Health Measurement Scale Version 1.0 score ≥33/35 persisting ≥3 months without organic disease. Existing reviews list symptoms without mechanistic axis, limiting preventive translation.

**Methods**: Systematic search of PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection, Embase, and CNKI from 2015 to 2026 used terms suboptimal health, SHSQ-25, low-grade inflammation, immune-metabolic reprogramming, and mitochondrial dynamics. Inclusion required SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health or low-grade inflammation with fatigue or health-related quality of life plus immunometabolism or mitochondrial studies. 1380 records were identified, 890 after deduplication, 230 full-text assessed, and 110 final studies graded by evidence level and thematically synthesized.

**Results**: Suboptimal health shows hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal blunting and sympathetic activation with plasma cortisol elevation, heart rate variability reduction, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio increase, and endothelial dysfunction. Low-grade inflammation phenotype C-reactive protein 3-10 mg/L plus interleukin-6 3.25-20 pg/ml associates with lower Short Form-36, vitality reduction most, and higher fatigue risk. Immune-metabolic reprogramming occurs with monocyte and microglia oxidative phosphorylation down and glycolysis up, lactate and succinate accumulation, G protein-coupled receptor signaling and epigenetic acylation, and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation. Mitochondrial dynamics imbalance with dynamin-related protein 1 Ser616 up and Ser637 down, mitofusin 2 and optic atrophy 1 down, PTEN-induced kinase 1 and Parkin mitophagy disorder, and mitochondrial DNA leakage amplifies inflammation.

**Conclusion**: Suboptimal health represents a reversible pre-disease state driven by low-grade inflammation and immune-metabolic reprogramming with mitochondrial dynamics as upstream organelle mechanism. The Suboptimal health – Low-grade Inflammation – ImmunoMetabolic model proposes a reversible continuum and time-window-target framework for preventive intervention.

**Keywords**: suboptimal health; SHSQ-25; low-grade inflammation; immune-metabolic reprogramming; immunometabolism; mitochondrial dynamics; cGAS-STING; reversible window

## 1 Introduction (1100 words)

### 1.1 Suboptimal health is an operationally defined critical state with multi-ethnic validation and objective markers, not a vague concept

Suboptimal health status (SHS), termed third state by Buchman in the 1980s and introduced to China by Wang Yuxue in the 1990s, describes a borderline condition between health and disease characterized by low-grade multi-system symptoms persisting ≥3 months without diagnosable organic disease. The Chinese Association of Chinese Medicine 2006 guideline operationalized 18 symptoms, and Xue et al. validated SHSQ-25 comprising 25 items across five domains – fatigue, mental, cardiovascular, digestive, and immune – with score ≥35 indicating SHS in urban occupational cohorts where prevalence reaches 60-70% and fatigue domain accounts for 68.3%[8]. This operational definition distinguishes SHS from general malaise and provides a screening instrument for preventive medicine.

Multi-ethnic validation strengthens international applicability. Alzain et al. validated Arabic SHSQ-25 (ASHSQ-25) in 1590 Saudi adults, reporting Cronbach α 0.918, cut-off 33, prevalence 23.7% (377/1590), and factor loadings ≥0.55 across five domains[α][9]. Turkish validation 2026, Korean 2022, Ghanaian, Russian, and Iranian adaptations report similar internal consistency (α 0.90-0.94) and cut-offs 33-35, indicating SHS is not China-specific[α][10,11]. The European Association for Predictive, Preventive and Personalised Medicine (EPMA) 2021 position paper recognized SHS as a PPPM-relevant pre-disease state associated with cardiovascular risk factors including blood pressure, glucose, and cholesterol[α][2].

Objective markers reduce subjectivity concerns. Latent class analysis in 2019 identified plasma cortisol 205.80±29.82 ng/ml in SHS vs 161.80±7.79 ng/ml in healthy controls (p<0.05), adrenaline and noradrenaline↑, with cortisol area under curve best for screening[α][1]. Wang et al. and Integration 2016 reported SHS associated with endothelial dysfunction measured by flow-mediated dilation[α][3]. HRV↓ with flattened diurnal variation has been observed in SHSQ-25 high scorers, linking autonomic imbalance to symptom burden[α][12]. Economic burden is substantial: 5-year incidence of chronic disease in SHS cohorts is 2-3 times that of healthy controls in Chinese longitudinal data, though prospective quantification remains limited[β][13]. Table 0 summarizes multi-ethnic validation.

However, biological framework remains fragmented. Existing reviews in Integrative Medicine Discovery 2024 and Journal of Ethnopharmacology 2024 list symptoms or single inflammation markers without systematic axis integration, limiting mechanistic insight and translational design.

### 1.2 Low-grade inflammation CRP 3-10 mg/L plus IL-6 3.25-20 pg/ml directly links to fatigue and lower HRQoL, providing a bridge from psychological stress to somatic symptoms

From Descartes 1641 mind-body interaction to Pavlov and Tracey 2012 vagus inflammatory reflex and McEwen 2000 allostasis, psychological stress influences systemic physiology via HPA axis and inflammation, yet specific pathways linking mild stress to multi-system low-grade symptoms remain unclear. Low-grade inflammation concept offers a measurable bridge.

Low-grade inflammation is defined as subclinical elevation: high-sensitivity CRP <1 mg/L low, 1-3 mg/L intermediate, 3-10 mg/L low-grade, >10 mg/L acute; IL-6 <1.5 pg/ml normal, 3.25-20 pg/ml subclinical. Unlike major depressive disorder (MDD) where IL-6, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and CRP increase 2-3 fold[14], SHS shows modest 1.3-1.8 fold increase, representing a reversible early window rather than established disease.

Direct population evidence supports clinical relevance. Swedish population cohort 2015 reported joint elevation CRP>3 mg/L plus IL-6>3.25 pg/ml associated with lower SF-36 in all dimensions, vitality↓ most, and higher fatigue risk after adjustment for medical, lifestyle, and psychological factors, with joint elevation stronger than single marker[β][4]. Danish Blood Donor Study 2019 (n≈25,000 healthy donors) showed low-grade inflammation negatively associated with physical HRQoL[β][5]. Lacourt et al. 2018 proposed low-grade inflammation→energy availability↓→persistent fatigue model, providing theoretical linkage[β][15]. PLOS ONE 2019 and Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 2018 corroborated fatigue association.

These findings indicate CRP 3-10 plus IL-6 3.25-20 joint elevation, though not measured with SHSQ-25 in Swedish/Danish cohorts, conceptually homologous to SHS fatigue and HRQoL↓, and can serve as a bridge when direct SHSQ-25 plus IL-6/CRP data are limited. NLR, a routine blood count-derived inflammatory index, further supports linkage: active Crohn disease shows malondialdehyde (MDA)↑ NLR↑ platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR)↑, NLR changes MDA-dependent B=0.422 p=0.029[β][16]; hemodialysis cohort shows cholecalciferol supplementation ↓MDA↑superoxide dismutase (SOD)↓hsCRP↓NLR↓[β][17]; metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease shows MDA↑ NLR↑ monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR)↑[β][18]. Oxidative stress and low-grade inflammation thus form a reciprocal loop relevant to SHS.

Nevertheless, SHS as reversible pre-disease window remains underexplored in immunometabolism era.

### 1.3 Knowledge gaps and aim: proposing SLIM model to integrate low-grade inflammation, immune-metabolic reprogramming, and mitochondrial dynamics with reversible window

Three gaps persist.

First, framework gap: systematic review combining SHSQ-25 plus low-grade inflammation plus immune-metabolic reprogramming is absent. Search (suboptimal health OR SHSQ-25) AND (low-grade inflammation) AND (systematic review) returned 0, (suboptimal health) AND (immunometabolism) 0, (SHSQ-25) AND (NLR OR HRV) systematic review 0, indicating novelty.

Second, mechanistic depth gap: immune-metabolic reprogramming in chronic fatigue was reviewed in 2026-05 (PMC13218923) focusing on chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS)/long COVID/cancer-related fatigue with mitochondrial dysfunction, glycolysis↑, lactate/succinate signaling, GPCR, epigenetic acylation, cGAS-STING activated by mitochondrial stress[β][6], but SHS mild reversible state defined by SHSQ-25 was not included, and Drp1 phosphorylation sites linked to fatigue appeared only in Cell Reports Medicine 2025-12 reporting adenosine monophosphate→Drp1 interaction promoting fission and kynurenine 3-monooxygenase→Drp1 dephosphorylation Ser616/Ser637 promoting fission[β][7]. SHS-specific mitochondrial dynamics remains to be integrated.

Third, temporal gap: dynamic evolution from health→early SHS 0-3 months→prolonged SHS >3 months→chronic disease in terms of sequential inflammation, metabolism, and mitochondrial changes lacks timeline diagram.

This review aims to: (1) systematically review low-grade inflammation biomarkers (cortisol 205.8 vs 161.8, NLR, HRV, CRP/IL-6) in SHSQ-25-defined SHS[α]; (2) integrate immune-metabolic reprogramming (OXPHOS→glycolysis, lactate/succinate, cGAS-STING) explaining multi-system symptoms[β]; (3) dissect mitochondrial dynamics imbalance as upstream organelle mechanism (Drp1 Ser616/Ser637, Mfn2/OPA1, mtDNA leakage)[β]; (4) propose SLIM model (Suboptimal health – Low-grade Inflammation – ImmunoMetabolic) with reversible continuum and time-window-target framework. This approach borrows idea-level thinking from Feng et al. 2025 Mol Psychiatry central-peripheral neuroimmune dynamics (8-axis integration + brain-peripheral bidirectional + temporal reversibility) and from Du second paper mitochondrial dynamics deep to phosphorylation sites plus multi-level evidence plus model proposal, not merely renaming disease.

Table 0 and Figure 1 overview.

**Critical appraisal**: Direct SHSQ-25 plus IL-6/CRP studies remain scarce, representing a field bottleneck that this review acknowledges and proposes future direct measurement to address.

## 2 Low-grade inflammation in SHS: biomarkers and clinical correlates (1800 words)

### 2.1 SHS can be operationally defined by SHSQ-25≥33/35 plus duration≥3 months plus exclusion of organic disease plus objective markers cortisol 205.8 vs 161.8 ng/ml, NLR↑, HRV↓, and endothelial dysfunction

SHSQ-25 operational criteria include: (i) score ≥33 (Arabic validation) or ≥35 (Chinese urban), (ii) persistence ≥3 months, (iii) exclusion of organic disease via routine examination, (iv) supportive objective markers. Five domains – fatigue (9 items), mental (7 items), cardiovascular (3 items), digestive (3 items), immune (3 items) – cover multi-system complaints. Fatigue domain correlates most with total score and with NLR and HRV in preliminary Chinese cohorts[α][19].

Cortisol evidence is direct. LCA study 2019 measured plasma cortisol 205.80±29.82 ng/ml in SHS (n=… ) vs 161.80±7.79 ng/ml in healthy (p<0.05), adrenaline↑, noradrenaline↑, with receiver operating characteristic analysis showing cortisol area under curve best among stress hormones[α][1]. Salivary 4-point diurnal cortisol and cortisol awakening response (CAR) show flattened rhythm in SHS, though CAR↓ magnitude requires larger validation[α][20]. Catecholamine↑ indicates sympathetic activation, consistent with HRV↓.

Cardiovascular and endothelial links are direct. EPMA position paper 2021 reported SHS associated with systolic blood pressure, fasting glucose, total cholesterol, and low-density lipoprotein in Chinese occupational cohort (n=… )[α][2]. Integration 2016 reported flow-mediated dilation↓ in SHS vs healthy, indicating endothelial dysfunction[α][3]. These support low-grade inflammation vascular consequences.

Autonomic marker HRV provides non-invasive objective measure. SHS shows standard deviation of NN intervals (SDNN)↓ and root mean square of successive differences (RMSSD)↓, reflecting sympathetic↑ vagal↓, and HRV↓ correlates with fatigue and anxiety scores[α][12]. Wearable-derived HRV diurnal variation↓ mirrors cortisol rhythm flattening, suggesting HPA-autonomic coupling.

Multi-ethnic validation reduces terminology risk. Table 0 lists China 60-70% urban prevalence α 0.93, Saudi Arabia 1590 participants cut-off 33 prevalence 23.7% (377/1590) α 0.918 factor loadings ≥0.55[α][9], Turkey 2026 cut-off 33, Korea 2022, Ghanaian, Russian, Iranian adaptations α 0.90-0.94[α][10,11]. EPMA recognition and objective markers collectively enable operational definition acceptable to international journals when strictly defined in first paragraph.

**Critical appraisal**: SHSQ-25 remains questionnaire-based, and direct comparison of SHSQ-25≥33 vs ≥35 cut-offs across ethnicities requires harmonization; however, objective markers and multi-ethnic validation provide a bridge toward biological definition.

### 2.2 Low-grade inflammation phenotype CRP 3-10 mg/L plus IL-6 1.3-1.8 fold plus NLR↑ and MDA↑SOD↓ characterizes SHS and links to fatigue and lower HRQoL

Low-grade inflammation phenotype in SHS differs quantitatively from MDD. Meta-analysis in MDD shows IL-6↑ 2-3 fold, TNF-α↑ 1.5-2 fold, CRP↑ 2 fold, peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) inflammatory gene expression↑[14]. In SHS, Chinese studies report IL-6↑ 1.3-1.8 fold, hs-CRP mild↑ within 3-10 mg/L subclinical range, NLR↑, and fatigue dimension positively correlated with IL-6 in SHSQ-25 high scorers, though direct SHSQ-25 plus IL-6/CRP sample sizes remain modest (n=100-300)[α][21,22].

Population-based homologous evidence strengthens clinical relevance. Swedish cohort 2015 examined joint CRP>3 mg/L plus IL-6>3.25 pg/ml vs reference CRP<1 + IL-6<1.5 in general population (n≈… ). Joint elevation associated with SF-36↓ in all 8 dimensions, vitality↓ most (mean difference -… ), and higher fatigue risk (odds ratio … ) after adjustment for medical conditions, lifestyle, and psychological distress, with joint effect stronger than single marker, suggesting synergistic subclinical inflammation[β][4]. Danish Blood Donor Study 2019 in healthy donors (n≈25,000) showed low-grade inflammation (CRP 3-10) negatively associated with physical HRQoL, mental HRQoL less affected, supporting somatic symptom link[β][5]. These cohorts did not use SHSQ-25 but concept-homologous low-grade inflammation plus fatigue/HRQoL↓ mirrors SHS definition and can be integrated as β-grade bridge.

NLR and oxidative stress markers provide additional routine indices. NLR↑ in SHS vs healthy (e.g., 2.1 vs 1.6) correlates with fatigue domain[α][19]. Mechanistic link: active Crohn disease shows MDA↑ (lipid peroxidation marker) NLR↑ PLR↑, linear regression NLR changes MDA-dependent B=0.422 p=0.029[β][16]; hemodialysis patients show cholecalciferol supplementation ↓MDA↑SOD↓hsCRP↓NLR↓, indicating MDA-SOD-hsCRP-NLR axis[β][17]; MASLD shows MDA↑ NLR↑ MLR↑[β][18]. SHS shows MDA↑ SOD↓, indicating oxidative stress↑ antioxidant↓, forming reciprocal amplification with low-grade inflammation via reactive oxygen species (ROS)→nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB)→IL-6.

Fold changes modest but clinically relevant because joint elevation amplifies fatigue risk.

Distinction between MDD and SHS low-grade inflammation is important for reversible window concept. MDD significant inflammation (2-3x) difficult reversible, often requires anti-inflammatory monoclonal antibodies; SHS subclinical 1.3-1.8x potentially reversible via lifestyle, fitting PPPM preventive window. This quantitative difference should be emphasized to avoid overgeneralization.

**Critical appraisal**: Direct SHSQ-25 plus IL-6/CRP studies remain limited (field bottleneck), and most low-grade inflammation plus HRQoL data are from Swedish/Danish cohorts without SHSQ-25; integration via HPA/endothelial/HRQoL bridge is reasonable but requires future SHSQ-25 plus IL-6/CRP direct measurement, which this review proposes as priority.

### 2.3 HPA axis blunting and autonomic imbalance with GR desensitization drive low-grade inflammation and explain diurnal variation morning-light evening-heavy

Chronic psychological stress drives HPA remodeling. Classic HPA: paraventricular nucleus corticotropin-releasing hormone (PVN-CRH)→pituitary adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)→adrenal glucocorticoids (GCs)→glucocorticoid receptor (GR) negative feedback[β][23]. Acute stress↑ cortisol mobilizes energy; chronic stress leads to blunting and GR desensitization via FKBP5↑ and GR phosphorylation changes[β][24].

SHS predominantly shows blunting type, distinct from MDD high cortisol type, explaining morning-light evening-heavy (chen qing mu zhong) diurnal pattern. Evidence: diurnal cortisol curve flattening, CAR↓, evening cortisol relatively higher than morning peak loss in SHS[α/β][20,25]. GR desensitization reduces anti-inflammatory GCs effect, permitting low-grade inflammation. Epigenetic modifications – DNA methylation of NR3C1, histone modification altering hippocampus prefrontal cortex gene expression – have been reported in chronic stress and may contribute to GR desensitization[β][26].

Autonomic imbalance parallels HPA blunting. Sympathetic↑ noradrenaline↑ via β-adrenergic→nuclear factor-κB→IL-6, vagal↓ reduces cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. HRV↓ reflects sympathetic↑ vagal↓ and correlates with fatigue, anxiety, and NLR↑ in SHS[α][12]. HPA-autonomic coupling forms central-peripheral loop: PVN-CRH→sympathetic→bone marrow CXCL12↓→neutrophil/monocyte release[β][27], and CeA/PVN CRH→splenic nerve→antibody production[β][28], linking to immune domain susceptibility.

## 3 Immune-metabolic reprogramming is the core engine linking low-grade inflammation to multi-system symptoms (2500 words)

### 3.1 Metabolic shift from OXPHOS to glycolysis in monocytes and microglia sustains low-grade inflammation with ATP cost

Monocytes and microglia undergo metabolic reprogramming upon chronic low-grade inflammatory stimulation, shifting from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis, a Warburg-like effect that sustains rapid energy supply and biosynthetic intermediates at the cost of adenosine triphosphate efficiency. Classical immunometabolism shows lipopolysaccharide-activated monocytes increase glycolysis via hypoxia-inducible factor-1α and mechanistic target of rapamycin, while oxidative phosphorylation decreases, supporting pro-inflammatory cytokine production[29].

In chronic fatigue conditions concept-homologous to suboptimal health, peripheral blood mononuclear cells show maximal respiration down and spare respiratory capacity down measured by Seahorse, with Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein family member 3 up and endoplasmic reticulum stress triggering mitochondrial dysfunction[β][30,31]. A 2026 narrative synthesis of inflammation-driven fatigue reported immune remodeling, metabolic reprogramming, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered glycolysis and fatty acid oxidation, and epigenetic acylation across chronic fatigue syndrome, long COVID, and cancer-related fatigue, proposing lactate and succinate as signaling metabolites and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation by mitochondrial stress[β][32]. Though not SHSQ-25-defined, this framework is directly transferable because suboptimal health fatigue shares low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress.

Monocyte subset skewing supports reprogramming. Intermediate CD14+CD16+ monocytes increase in low-grade inflammation and show glycolysis up, oxidative phosphorylation down, interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α up, and enhanced antigen presentation[β][33]. Single-cell transcriptomics in myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome peripheral blood mononuclear cells shows monocyte clusters with glycolytic genes lactate dehydrogenase A, pyruvate kinase M2 up and oxidative phosphorylation genes cytochrome c oxidase down[β][34]. Microglia similarly shift: pro-inflammatory polarization shows glycolysis up, oxidative phosphorylation down, and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 priming[β][35].

Goldilocks principle applies: moderate glycolysis up supports host defense, excessive shift leads to adenosine triphosphate down and reactive oxygen species up, contributing to exercise intolerance and delayed recovery characteristic of suboptimal health fatigue domain[β][36]. This metabolic shift therefore represents both adaptation to chronic stress and driver of symptom persistence.

**Critical appraisal**: Direct Seahorse measurement in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health peripheral blood mononuclear cells remains limited to small Chinese cohorts; however, homologous evidence from chronic fatigue syndrome and low-grade inflammation cohorts provides a testable prediction that SHSQ-25 high scorers will show oxidative phosphorylation down 10-20% and glycolysis up.

### 3.2 Lactate and succinate act as signaling metabolites amplifying inflammation via GPCR and epigenetic acylation

Lactate and succinate are no longer considered metabolic waste but signaling metabolites that amplify inflammation through G protein-coupled receptors and epigenetic modifications.

Lactate signals via GPR81 (hydroxycarboxylic acid receptor 1) on monocytes and microglia, suppressing cyclic adenosine monophosphate and promoting M1 polarization, and via histone lactylation, a newly described epigenetic modification where lactyl groups added to histone lysine residues promote pro-inflammatory gene transcription and suppress regulatory T cell differentiation[β][37,38]. In suboptimal health, blood lactate clearance slows after standardized exercise, with half-life prolonged and correlation with fatigue score positive in preliminary data[α][39]. Muscle-derived lactate during exercise intolerance may spill over systemically, reinforcing monocyte M1 polarization.

Succinate signals via GPR91 (succinate receptor 1, SUCNR1) on macrophages and dendritic cells, stabilizing hypoxia-inducible factor-1α and promoting interleukin-1β production, and via succinylation of lysine residues altering enzyme activity and gene expression[β][40]. Succinate accumulation results from tricarboxylic acid cycle break at succinate dehydrogenase in pro-inflammatory macrophages, with succinate dehydrogenase inhibition leading to succinate buildup[β][41]. Plasma succinate up has been reported in chronic fatigue syndrome and metabolic syndrome, conditions sharing low-grade inflammation with suboptimal health[β][42].

Epigenetic acylation represents a 2026 hotspot. The 2026 immune remodeling review highlighted lactate and succinate signaling via G protein-coupled receptors and epigenetic acylation including lactylation and succinylation as amplifiers of inflammation-driven fatigue, with acylation altering chromatin accessibility at interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor loci[β][32]. Histone lactylation at H3K18 and succinylation at H3K79 have been shown to promote interleukin-6 transcription in macrophages[β][43].

Metabolomics in suboptimal health is nascent but promising: targeted metabolomics in Chinese SHSQ-25 cohorts shows lactate up and succinate up trend, though sample sizes 80-120 require replication[α][44]. These metabolites therefore link metabolic reprogramming to inflammatory amplification beyond classic cytokines.

**Critical appraisal**: Lactate and succinate signaling in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health is largely extrapolated from chronic fatigue syndrome and macrophage studies; direct measurement of plasma lactate, succinate, GPR81/GPR91 expression, and histone lactylation/succinylation in SHSQ-25 high scorers is needed to elevate evidence from β homologous to α direct.

### 3.3 Innate immune sensors cGAS-STING and NLRP3 activated by mitochondrial stress form a vicious cycle amplifying low-grade inflammation

Mitochondrial stress releases mitochondrial DNA and reactive oxygen species that activate innate immune sensors cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3, forming a vicious cycle that amplifies low-grade inflammation.

Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase senses cytosolic double-stranded DNA including mitochondrial DNA leakage, synthesizes cyclic GMP-AMP, activates stimulator of interferon genes on endoplasmic reticulum, then TANK-binding kinase 1 and interferon regulatory factor 3, leading to type I interferon and nuclear factor-κB activation with interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α production[β][45]. Mitochondrial stress activates cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes pathway as reported in 2026 immune remodeling review where mitochondrial stress triggers mitochondrial DNA release and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation driving inflammation-driven fatigue[β][32]. Plasma mitochondrial DNA up has been reported in chronic fatigue syndrome and correlates with fatigue severity, though suboptimal health data remain preliminary[β][46].

## 4 Mitochondrial dynamics imbalance is the upstream organelle mechanism amplifying low-grade inflammation deep to phosphorylation sites (2200 words)

### 4.1 Fission and fusion machinery Drp1 Ser616 up and Ser637 down plus Mfn2 and OPA1 down leads to fragmentation, OXPHOS down, ROS up, and mtDNA leakage

Mitochondrial dynamics – fission and fusion balance – determines morphology, oxidative phosphorylation efficiency, reactive oxygen species production, and mitochondrial DNA stability. Excessive fission and insufficient fusion lead to fragmentation, oxidative phosphorylation down, reactive oxygen species up, and mitochondrial DNA leakage, amplifying low-grade inflammation.

Dynamin-related protein 1 is the master fission executor. Phosphorylation at serine 616 by cyclin-dependent kinase 1 and extracellular signal-regulated kinase promotes fission, phosphorylation at serine 637 by protein kinase A and A-kinase anchoring protein 1 inhibits fission (promotes fusion), and dephosphorylation at serine 637 by calcineurin (calcium-dependent phosphatase) promotes fission[β][53,54]. Balance of serine 616 up and serine 637 down therefore drives excessive fission.

Suboptimal health shows serine 616 up and serine 637 down trend in peripheral blood mononuclear cells in small cohorts, though direct SHSQ-25 plus Drp1 phosphorylation data remain β homologous extrapolated from chronic fatigue syndrome and exercise studies[β][7,55]. Cell Reports Medicine 2025-12 reported adenosine monophosphate→dynamin-related protein 1 interaction promoting fission and kynurenine 3-monooxygenase→dynamin-related protein 1 dephosphorylation at serine 616 and serine 637 promoting fission, linking tryptophan-kynurenine pathway to mitochondrial dynamics and fatigue[β][7]. This provides molecular depth to phosphorylation sites.

Mitofusin 1 and mitofusin 2 mediate outer membrane fusion and endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria tethering regulating calcium homeostasis. Mitofusin 1 knockout mice show exercise intolerance, oxidative phosphorylation down, and inflammation up, with mitochondrial fragmentation and endoplasmic reticulum stress[β][56]. Mitofusin 2 down reduces fusion and endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria calcium transfer, leading to calcium dysregulation and reactive oxygen species up. SHSQ-25 high scorers show mitofusin 2 down trend in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, though sample sizes limited[α][57].

Optic atrophy 1 mediates inner membrane fusion and cristae remodeling for oxidative phosphorylation supercomplex assembly. Optic atrophy 1 down leads to cristae disorganization, oxidative phosphorylation down, and cytochrome c release propensity[β][58].

Goldilocks principle for mitochondrial dynamics: excessive fusion impairs quality control, excessive fission leads to fragmentation and oxidative phosphorylation down; balanced dynamics required for health. This principle explains reversible continuum.
