# Verified SHS original evidence and PDF-audit addendum — Scheme B — 2026-10-15

## Scope and evidence rules

This addendum records two linked updates:

1. four user-supplied SHS context/multi-omics PDFs were audited locally for exact identity, page count, SHA-256 and abstract/table-level findings; and
2. two additional human SHSQ-25 molecular/oxidative records were independently verified online from PubMed/PMC metadata and abstracts, but were not presented as local PDF-audited records because their PDFs were not supplied in this batch.

These records are not pooled with the direct SHS–cortisol estimates. They are assigned to context, molecular bridge or pregnancy-specific oxidative/vascular bridge roles. None demonstrates mitochondrial dynamics mediation, cGAS–STING activation or NLRP3 activation in SHSQ-25-defined participants.

### C1 — SHS and endothelial function

The supplied PDF abstract (PDF p. 1) describes a community-based cross-sectional study of 459 residents of Samara, Russia. SHSQ-25 scores were examined with cardiovascular risk measures and endothelial-function indicators. The abstract reports correlations between the endothelial-function index and overall SHS (`r = −0.31, p < 0.05`), fatigue (`r = −0.36, p < 0.05`), mental status (`r = −0.29, p < 0.05`) and the cardiovascular domain (`r = −0.36`). Table 1 is on PDF p. 3, Table 2 on p. 4 and Table 3 on p. 4. This is an objective vascular bridge, not a cortisol or mitochondrial estimate.

### C2 — SHS and IgG N-glycomics

The supplied PDF abstract (PDF p. 1) describes a case–control study of 124 SHS participants and 124 age-, sex- and BMI-matched healthy controls. IgG N-glycan profiles from 248 plasma samples were analysed by ultra-performance liquid chromatography. After adjustment for age, education, physical activity, income, depression score, fasting glucose and LDL cholesterol, 16 IgG N-glycan traits were associated with SHS at 5% FDR. Glycan peaks GP20, GP9 and GP12 were related to SHS domains, and the tenfold cross-validated model had mean AUC 0.703 (95% CI 0.637–0.768). Participant characteristics are in Table 1 (PDF p. 6); model development/results are on PDF pp. 7–9. This supports a candidate glycomic biomarker layer, not proof of immune-metabolic reprogramming or inflammation causality.

### C3 — SHSQ-25 network structure

The supplied PDF abstract (PDF p. 1) describes a cross-sectional study of 217 Ghanaian participants aged 30–80 years, approximately 61% female. The median SHS score separated optimal and suboptimal groups. Graphical LASSO and multidimensional-scaling methods were used. The abstract reports 58 non-zero edges in the optimal network versus 43 in the suboptimal network (`p = 0.024`), with fatigue central in the suboptimal population and the cardiovascular domain most relevant in the optimal population. Summary/descriptive tables are on PDF pp. 5–6. This supports systems-level phenotype description, not temporal or causal network inference.

### C4 — Caregiving and SHS

The supplied PDF abstract (PDF p. 1) describes 368 Australian participants: 203 unpaid carers and 165 general-population controls. SHS prevalence was 43.0% (98/203) in carers versus 12.7% (21/165) in controls (`p < 0.001`); the adjusted association was aOR 6.400 (95% CI 3.751–10.919). Participant characteristics are in Table 2 (PDF pp. 6–7), and SHSQ-25/DASS-21 analyses are reported in Tables 3–5 (PDF pp. 8–11). This is psychosocial risk-stratification context, not cortisol, inflammatory or mitochondrial mediation.

## B. Additional online-verified human molecular/oxidative records

### M1 — SHS and telomere length

**Exact title:** *Telomere Length and Accelerated Biological Aging in the China Suboptimal Health Cohort: A Case-Control Study*
**PMID/PubMed:** [28617662](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28617662/)
**DOI:** [10.1089/omi.2017.0050](https://doi.org/10.1089/omi.2017.0050)

The PubMed abstract reports 294 SHS cases and 294 ideal-health controls matched by age, sex and BMI. SHS was measured with SHSQ-25 and relative telomere length by quantitative PCR. Shorter telomere length was reported in SHS; adjusted odds ratios for the first, second and third telomere-length quartiles versus the longest quartile were 3.81 (95% CI 2.21–6.56), 2.84 (1.65–4.90) and 1.71 (1.00–2.94), respectively, and SHS score correlated negatively with relative telomere length (`r = −0.218`).

**Locked role:** direct molecular bridge for cellular ageing/replicative stress. It does not measure Drp1, MFN2, OPA1, PINK1/Parkin, mtDNA, cGAS–STING, NLRP3, CRP or IL-6, and it is not evidence of mitochondrial-dynamics mediation. Online-verified only until the original PDF is supplied.

### M2 — SHS, oxidative stress and placental/angiogenic markers in pregnancy

**Exact title:** *Placental lesions and differential expression of pro-and anti-angiogenic growth mediators and oxidative DNA damage marker in placentae of Ghanaian suboptimal and optimal health status pregnant women who later developed preeclampsia*
**PMID/PubMed:** [35312727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35312727/)
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0265717](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265717)
**Full text:** [PMC8936490](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8936490/)

The PMC full text reports a nested case–control analysis originating from 593 normotensive singleton pregnancies assessed at 10–20 weeks using SHSQ-25. A median score of 19 separated SHS from optimal health in that cohort. The study examined serum oxidative-stress and angiogenic mediators and placental histopathology/immunohistochemistry among women who later developed preeclampsia. The paper reports differential oxidative DNA-damage and pro-/anti-angiogenic patterns in the SHS-embodied pregnancy groups.

**Locked role:** pregnancy-specific oxidative/vascular bridge and tissue-level context. It must not be generalized to non-pregnant SHS, treated as evidence of mitochondrial dynamics, or pooled with the cortisol studies. Online/PMC verified; a local PDF hash and byte-level audit are pending because the original PDF was not supplied in this batch.

### M3 — COACS cohort provenance

**Exact title:** *China suboptimal health cohort study: rationale, design and baseline characteristics*
**PMID/PubMed:** [27737677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27737677/)
**DOI:** [10.1186/s12967-016-1046-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-016-1046-y)
**Full text:** [PMC5064923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5064923/)

The published cohort-design paper describes COACS as a community-based prospective study in Caofeidian, Tangshan, China. It reports 4,313 included baseline participants and a SHS prevalence of 9.0% using a score threshold of 35. This record is useful for tracing later COACS molecular studies, but it is a design/baseline paper rather than a direct mitochondrial or inflammatory endpoint study.

## Evidence boundary

The candidate search reported no eligible SHSQ-25 human records directly measuring Drp1, MFN2, OPA1, PINK1/Parkin, mtDNA release, cGAS–STING, NLRP3 or strict HRV endpoints. This should be described as a finding of the present candidate search, not as proof that no such paper exists until the search strategy, dates, databases and export are archived. The current review therefore uses M1/M2 as direct molecular/oxidative bridges and retains mitochondrial dynamics, cGAS–STING and NLRP3 as transfer hypotheses and prospective research priorities.
