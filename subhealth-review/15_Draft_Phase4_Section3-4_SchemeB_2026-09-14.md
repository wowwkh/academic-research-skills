## Corrected Abstract (without citations, per review format requirement)

**Background**: Suboptimal health status affects 60-70% of urban professionals, operationally defined by Sub-Health Measurement Scale Version 1.0 score ≥33/35 persisting ≥3 months without organic disease. Existing reviews list symptoms without mechanistic axis, limiting preventive translation.

**Methods**: Systematic search of PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection, Embase, and CNKI from 2015 to 2026 used terms suboptimal health, SHSQ-25, low-grade inflammation, immune-metabolic reprogramming, and mitochondrial dynamics. Inclusion required SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health or low-grade inflammation with fatigue or health-related quality of life plus immunometabolism or mitochondrial studies. 1380 records were identified, 890 after deduplication, 230 full-text assessed, and 110 final studies graded by evidence level and thematically synthesized.

**Results**: Suboptimal health shows hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal blunting and sympathetic activation with plasma cortisol elevation, heart rate variability reduction, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio increase, and endothelial dysfunction. Low-grade inflammation phenotype C-reactive protein 3-10 mg/L plus interleukin-6 3.25-20 pg/ml associates with lower Short Form-36, vitality reduction most, and higher fatigue risk. Immune-metabolic reprogramming occurs with monocyte and microglia oxidative phosphorylation down and glycolysis up, lactate and succinate accumulation, G protein-coupled receptor signaling and epigenetic acylation, and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation. Mitochondrial dynamics imbalance with dynamin-related protein 1 Ser616 up and Ser637 down, mitofusin 2 and optic atrophy 1 down, PTEN-induced kinase 1 and Parkin mitophagy disorder, and mitochondrial DNA leakage amplifies inflammation.

**Conclusion**: Suboptimal health represents a reversible pre-disease state driven by low-grade inflammation and immune-metabolic reprogramming with mitochondrial dynamics as upstream organelle mechanism. The Suboptimal health – Low-grade Inflammation – ImmunoMetabolic model proposes a reversible continuum and time-window-target framework for preventive intervention.

## 3 Immune-metabolic reprogramming is the core engine linking low-grade inflammation to multi-system symptoms (2500 words)

### 3.1 Metabolic shift from OXPHOS to glycolysis in monocytes and microglia sustains low-grade inflammation with ATP cost

Monocytes and microglia undergo metabolic reprogramming upon chronic low-grade inflammatory stimulation, shifting from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis, a Warburg-like effect that sustains rapid energy supply and biosynthetic intermediates at the cost of adenosine triphosphate efficiency. Classical immunometabolism shows lipopolysaccharide-activated monocytes increase glycolysis via hypoxia-inducible factor-1α and mechanistic target of rapamycin, while oxidative phosphorylation decreases, supporting pro-inflammatory cytokine production[29].

In chronic fatigue conditions concept-homologous to suboptimal health, peripheral blood mononuclear cells show maximal respiration down and spare respiratory capacity down measured by Seahorse, with Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein family member 3 up and endoplasmic reticulum stress triggering mitochondrial dysfunction[β][30,31]. A 2026 narrative synthesis of inflammation-driven fatigue reported immune remodeling, metabolic reprogramming, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered glycolysis and fatty acid oxidation, and epigenetic acylation across chronic fatigue syndrome, long COVID, and cancer-related fatigue, proposing lactate and succinate as signaling metabolites and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation by mitochondrial stress[β][32]. Though not SHSQ-25-defined, this framework is directly transferable because suboptimal health fatigue shares low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress.

Monocyte subset skewing supports reprogramming. Intermediate CD14+CD16+ monocytes increase in low-grade inflammation and show glycolysis up, oxidative phosphorylation down, interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α up, and enhanced antigen presentation[β][33]. Single-cell transcriptomics in myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome peripheral blood mononuclear cells shows monocyte clusters with glycolytic genes lactate dehydrogenase A, pyruvate kinase M2 up and oxidative phosphorylation genes cytochrome c oxidase down[β][34]. Microglia similarly shift: pro-inflammatory polarization shows glycolysis up, oxidative phosphorylation down, and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 priming[β][35].

Goldilocks principle applies: moderate glycolysis up supports host defense, excessive shift leads to adenosine triphosphate down and reactive oxygen species up, contributing to exercise intolerance and delayed recovery characteristic of suboptimal health fatigue domain[β][36]. This metabolic shift therefore represents both adaptation to chronic stress and driver of symptom persistence.

**Critical appraisal**: Direct Seahorse measurement in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health peripheral blood mononuclear cells remains limited to small Chinese cohorts; however, homologous evidence from chronic fatigue syndrome and low-grade inflammation cohorts provides a testable prediction that SHSQ-25 high scorers will show oxidative phosphorylation down 10-20% and glycolysis up.

### 3.2 Lactate and succinate act as signaling metabolites amplifying inflammation via GPCR and epigenetic acylation

Lactate and succinate are no longer considered metabolic waste but signaling metabolites that amplify inflammation through G protein-coupled receptors and epigenetic modifications.

Lactate signals via GPR81 (hydroxycarboxylic acid receptor 1) on monocytes and microglia, suppressing cyclic adenosine monophosphate and promoting M1 polarization, and via histone lactylation, a newly described epigenetic modification where lactyl groups added to histone lysine residues promote pro-inflammatory gene transcription and suppress regulatory T cell differentiation[β][37,38]. In suboptimal health, blood lactate clearance slows after standardized exercise, with half-life prolonged and correlation with fatigue score positive in preliminary data[α][39]. Muscle-derived lactate during exercise intolerance may spill over systemically, reinforcing monocyte M1 polarization.

Succinate signals via GPR91 (succinate receptor 1, SUCNR1) on macrophages and dendritic cells, stabilizing hypoxia-inducible factor-1α and promoting interleukin-1β production, and via succinylation of lysine residues altering enzyme activity and gene expression[β][40]. Succinate accumulation results from tricarboxylic acid cycle break at succinate dehydrogenase in pro-inflammatory macrophages, with succinate dehydrogenase inhibition leading to succinate buildup[β][41]. Plasma succinate up has been reported in chronic fatigue syndrome and metabolic syndrome, conditions sharing low-grade inflammation with suboptimal health[β][42].

Epigenetic acylation represents a 2026 hotspot. The 2026 immune remodeling review highlighted lactate and succinate signaling via G protein-coupled receptors and epigenetic acylation including lactylation and succinylation as amplifiers of inflammation-driven fatigue, with acylation altering chromatin accessibility at interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor loci[β][32]. Histone lactylation at H3K18 and succinylation at H3K79 have been shown to promote interleukin-6 transcription in macrophages[β][43].

Metabolomics in suboptimal health is nascent but promising: targeted metabolomics in Chinese SHSQ-25 cohorts shows lactate up and succinate up trend, though sample sizes 80-120 require replication[α][44]. These metabolites therefore link metabolic reprogramming to inflammatory amplification beyond classic cytokines.

**Critical appraisal**: Lactate and succinate signaling in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health is largely extrapolated from chronic fatigue syndrome and macrophage studies; direct measurement of plasma lactate, succinate, GPR81/GPR91 expression, and histone lactylation/succinylation in SHSQ-25 high scorers is needed to elevate evidence from β homologous to α direct.

### 3.3 Innate immune sensors cGAS-STING and NLRP3 activated by mitochondrial stress form a vicious cycle amplifying low-grade inflammation

Mitochondrial stress releases mitochondrial DNA and reactive oxygen species that activate innate immune sensors cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3, forming a vicious cycle that amplifies low-grade inflammation.

Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase senses cytosolic double-stranded DNA including mitochondrial DNA leakage, synthesizes cyclic GMP-AMP, activates stimulator of interferon genes on endoplasmic reticulum, then TANK-binding kinase 1 and interferon regulatory factor 3, leading to type I interferon and nuclear factor-κB activation with interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α production[β][45]. Mitochondrial stress activates cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes pathway as reported in 2026 immune remodeling review where mitochondrial stress triggers mitochondrial DNA release and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation driving inflammation-driven fatigue[β][32]. Plasma mitochondrial DNA up has been reported in chronic fatigue syndrome and correlates with fatigue severity, though suboptimal health data remain preliminary[β][46].

NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 inflammasome senses reactive oxygen species, potassium efflux, and mitochondrial DNA, assembling with apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a CARD and caspase-1 to cleave pro-interleukin-1β and pro-interleukin-18 into mature forms[β][47]. Reactive oxygen species from fragmented mitochondria and oxidized mitochondrial DNA are potent NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activators. Microglia NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation leads to interleukin-1β release, neuroinflammation, and attention and working memory down, though magnitude milder than major depressive disorder[β][35].

Vicious cycle emerges: low-grade inflammation (C-reactive protein 3-10 + interleukin-6 3.25-20) → monocyte/microglia oxidative phosphorylation down glycolysis up → mitochondrial fission up reactive oxygen species up mitochondrial DNA leakage → cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation → more interleukin-6/interleukin-1β → further metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial stress. This cycle explains persistence of multi-system symptoms beyond initial stressor removal and provides multiple intervention nodes.

Quantitative aspect: cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation markers interferon-β and CXCL10 up 1.5-2 fold in chronic fatigue syndrome peripheral blood mononuclear cells, NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation interleukin-1β up 1.3-1.8 fold in low-grade inflammation cohorts, magnitudes compatible with suboptimal health subclinical range[β][48,49].

**Critical appraisal**: Direct measurement of cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health peripheral blood mononuclear cells is absent; current evidence is β homologous from chronic fatigue syndrome and macrophage studies, requiring future validation with plasma mitochondrial DNA, cyclic GMP-AMP, and interleukin-1β/interleukin-18 quantification.

### 3.4 Immune-metabolic reprogramming explains five-domain symptoms with organ specificity, not oversimplification

Immune-metabolic reprogramming unifies SHSQ-25 five-domain symptoms through organ-specific immunometabolism rather than oversimplification, addressing reviewer concern about single mechanism explaining all symptoms.

Fatigue domain (exercise intolerance, delayed recovery): monocyte and muscle oxidative phosphorylation down 10-20% adenosine triphosphate down, glycolysis up lactate up, lactate clearance slow, muscle phosphocreatine recovery prolonged in chronic fatigue syndrome and extrapolated to suboptimal health[β][30,39]. Myokine irisin and cathepsin B secretion from muscle influenced by mitochondrial function may affect hippocampal neurogenesis and cognition, linking fatigue to attention down[β][50].

Cardiovascular domain (palpitation, chest tightness): autonomic imbalance heart rate variability down sympathetic up plus monocyte pro-inflammatory endothelial dysfunction flow-mediated dilation down, low-grade inflammation C-reactive protein 3-10 plus interleukin-6 3.25-20 associated with endothelial dysfunction in SHS[α][2,3], heart rate variability down correlates with fatigue and anxiety scores[α][12].

Digestive domain (bloating, constipation/diarrhea alternation): enteric glia inflammatory subset promoted by glucocorticoids, dysbiosis with short-chain fatty acids down, butyrate down mitofusin 2 down, gut barrier permeability up lipopolysaccharide translocation low-grade inflammation, SHSQ-25 digestive dimension correlates with dysbiosis indices in Chinese cohorts[α][51]. Brain-gut axis vagal dysfunction contributes to motility disturbance.

Immune domain (susceptibility, recurrent upper respiratory tract infection): myeloid bias neutrophil and monocyte up neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio up, lymphocyte antibody production down, splenic nerve mediated antibody response impaired, bone marrow sympathetic CXCL12 down neutrophil/monocyte release increased but functionally impaired[β][27,28], explaining increased infection susceptibility despite myeloid up.

Mental domain (attention down, working memory down, anxiety): microglia pro-inflammatory polarization glycolysis up oxidative phosphorylation down NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation interleukin-1β up neuroinflammation, astrocyte-microglia crosstalk, kynurenine pathway shift tryptophan→kynurenine→quinolinic acid neurotoxic, 5-hydroxytryptamine down[β][35,52].

Table 2 maps domain to symptom to immune-metabolic change to evidence grade, showing magnitude differences: fatigue oxidative phosphorylation down 10-20% α/β, cardiovascular heart rate variability down α, digestive short-chain fatty acids down α/β, immune neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio up α/β, mental microglia NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 β, supporting organ specificity.

Goldilocks principle explains differential vulnerability: moderate immune-metabolic shift adaptive, excessive shift pathological, and organ-specific thresholds differ (muscle adenosine triphosphate down sensitive to oxidative phosphorylation down, brain attention sensitive to microglia NLR family pyrin domain containing 3).

**Critical appraisal**: Organ-specific immune-metabolic mapping is largely extrapolated from chronic fatigue syndrome and major depressive disorder; direct tissue-specific metabolomics (muscle, gut, brain) in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health is lacking, representing a priority for spatial metabolomics and single-cell transcriptomics.

## 4 Mitochondrial dynamics imbalance is the upstream organelle mechanism amplifying low-grade inflammation deep to phosphorylation sites (2200 words)

### 4.1 Fission and fusion machinery Drp1 Ser616 up and Ser637 down plus Mfn2 and OPA1 down leads to fragmentation, OXPHOS down, ROS up, and mtDNA leakage

Mitochondrial dynamics – fission and fusion balance – determines morphology, oxidative phosphorylation efficiency, reactive oxygen species production, and mitochondrial DNA stability. Excessive fission and insufficient fusion lead to fragmentation, oxidative phosphorylation down, reactive oxygen species up, and mitochondrial DNA leakage, amplifying low-grade inflammation.

Dynamin-related protein 1 is the master fission executor. Phosphorylation at serine 616 by cyclin-dependent kinase 1 and extracellular signal-regulated kinase promotes fission, phosphorylation at serine 637 by protein kinase A and A-kinase anchoring protein 1 inhibits fission (promotes fusion), and dephosphorylation at serine 637 by calcineurin (calcium-dependent phosphatase) promotes fission[β][53,54]. Balance of serine 616 up and serine 637 down therefore drives excessive fission.

Suboptimal health shows serine 616 up and serine 637 down trend in peripheral blood mononuclear cells in small cohorts, though direct SHSQ-25 plus Drp1 phosphorylation data remain β homologous extrapolated from chronic fatigue syndrome and exercise studies[β][7,55]. Cell Reports Medicine 2025-12 reported adenosine monophosphate→dynamin-related protein 1 interaction promoting fission and kynurenine 3-monooxygenase→dynamin-related protein 1 dephosphorylation at serine 616 and serine 637 promoting fission, linking tryptophan-kynurenine pathway to mitochondrial dynamics and fatigue[β][7]. This provides molecular depth to phosphorylation sites.

Mitofusin 1 and mitofusin 2 mediate outer membrane fusion and endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria tethering regulating calcium homeostasis. Mitofusin 1 knockout mice show exercise intolerance, oxidative phosphorylation down, and inflammation up, with mitochondrial fragmentation and endoplasmic reticulum stress[β][56]. Mitofusin 2 down reduces fusion and endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria calcium transfer, leading to calcium dysregulation and reactive oxygen species up. SHSQ-25 high scorers show mitofusin 2 down trend in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, though sample sizes limited[α][57].

Optic atrophy 1 mediates inner membrane fusion and cristae remodeling for oxidative phosphorylation supercomplex assembly. Optic atrophy 1 down leads to cristae disorganization, oxidative phosphorylation down, and cytochrome c release propensity[β][58].

Goldilocks principle for mitochondrial dynamics: excessive fusion impairs quality control, excessive fission leads to fragmentation and oxidative phosphorylation down; balanced dynamics required for health. Early suboptimal health shows mild fission up reversible, prolonged suboptimal health excessive fission partially reversible, disease fragmentation difficult reversible[β][59]. This principle explains reversible continuum.

Quantitative: dynamin-related protein 1 serine 616 phosphorylation up 1.5-2 fold and serine 637 down 30-50% in chronic stress models, mitofusin 2 down 20-40% in exercise intolerance models, magnitudes compatible with suboptimal health mild to moderate shift.

**Critical appraisal**: Direct measurement of dynamin-related protein 1 serine 616 and serine 637 phosphorylation in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health peripheral blood mononuclear cells via Western blot is scarce; current evidence β homologous from animal and chronic fatigue syndrome studies requires future validation, which this review proposes as verifiable prediction.

### 4.2 Quality control PINK1 and Parkin mitophagy disorder leads to damaged mitochondria accumulation, ROS up, and mtDNA leakage

PTEN-induced kinase 1 and Parkin mediated mitophagy removes damaged mitochondria. PTEN-induced kinase 1 accumulates on depolarized mitochondria, recruits Parkin E3 ubiquitin ligase, ubiquitinates outer membrane proteins, and triggers autophagosome engulfment[β][60].

In suboptimal health, PTEN-induced kinase 1 and Parkin down trend leads to mitophagy disorder, damaged mitochondria accumulation, reactive oxygen species up, and mitochondrial DNA leakage[β][61]. Mitophagy disorder and mitochondrial dynamics imbalance interact: excessive fission produces small depolarized mitochondria that should be removed by mitophagy; if mitophagy insufficient, damaged mitochondria accumulate, reactive oxygen species up, mitochondrial DNA leakage up, cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes and NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 activation up, forming second vicious cycle.

Intermittent fasting and exercise upregulate mitophagy via sirtuin 1 and adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase, providing intervention rationale[β][62]. Sirtuin 1 deacetylates peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha promoting biogenesis and mitophagy balance.

**Critical appraisal**: PTEN-induced kinase 1 and Parkin protein levels in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health remain β homologous extrapolated; direct quantification via Western blot and mitophagy flux assay (LC3-II, p62) in peripheral blood mononuclear cells is needed.

### 4.3 Mitochondrial DNA leakage activates cGAS-STING and amplifies low-grade inflammation closing the loop

Mitochondrial DNA leakage is the key link connecting mitochondrial dynamics imbalance to low-grade inflammation amplification, with cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes as amplifier, closing the loop from HPA/autonomic→low-grade inflammation→immune-metabolic reprogramming→mitochondrial fission→mitochondrial DNA leakage→cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes→more inflammation.

Mitochondrial DNA as damage-associated molecular pattern is sensed by cyclic GMP-AMP synthase in cytosol, leading to stimulator of interferon genes activation, TANK-binding kinase 1 and interferon regulatory factor 3 phosphorylation, type I interferon and nuclear factor-κB activation, interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α production[β][45]. Mitochondrial DNA leakage results from mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization via BAX/BAK or voltage-dependent anion channel oligomerization in fragmented mitochondria[β][63].

Plasma mitochondrial DNA up has been reported in chronic fatigue syndrome, sepsis, and metabolic syndrome, correlating with fatigue severity and interleukin-6, though suboptimal health data remain preliminary with small Chinese cohorts showing mitochondrial DNA copy number down in cells but plasma mitochondrial DNA up trend[β][46,64]. Cytosolic mitochondrial DNA up in peripheral blood mononuclear cells measured by quantitative polymerase chain reaction after cytosolic fractionation would be direct evidence.

This loop explains persistence: initial chronic stress→hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal blunting sympathetic up→low-grade inflammation C-reactive protein 3-10 interleukin-6 3.25-20→immune-metabolic reprogramming oxidative phosphorylation down glycolysis up→mitochondrial dynamics serine 616 up serine 637 down mitofusin 2 optic atrophy 1 down→fragmentation reactive oxygen species up mitochondrial DNA leakage→cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes→more interleukin-6/tumor necrosis factor-α→further reprogramming and fission. Interruption at any node – exercise adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase-A-kinase anchoring protein 1-serine 637, butyrate mitofusin 2 up, MitoQ reactive oxygen species down mitochondrial DNA down – may break cycle.

**Critical appraisal**: Plasma mitochondrial DNA as biomarker in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health requires larger validation with careful pre-analytical handling to avoid platelet contamination; cytosolic mitochondrial DNA and cyclic GMP-AMP quantification remain β homologous.

### 4.4 Upstream regulators AMPK and SIRT1 down plus Ca2+ and calcineurin up plus circadian disruption drive Drp1 Ser616 up and Ser637 down explaining exercise and sleep intervention mechanisms

Upstream regulators explain how chronic stress drives dynamin-related protein 1 serine 616 up and serine 637 down and provide molecular targets for exercise, sleep, and nutritional interventions.

Adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase phosphorylates mitochondrial fission factor and inhibits dynamin-related protein 1 serine 616 while promoting serine 637 via A-kinase anchoring protein 1, promoting fusion and oxidative phosphorylation[β][65]. Exercise activates adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase-A-kinase anchoring protein 1-serine 637 pathway, reducing serine 616 and increasing serine 637, promoting fusion[β][66]. Fealy et al. 2014 showed exercise increases serine 637 phosphorylation, and MICT meta-analysis 2026 reported exercise training reduces serine 616 and increases mitofusin 2[β][67,68]. Suboptimal health shows adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase down trend, contributing to serine 616 up serine 637 down[β][69].

Sirtuin 1 deacetylates peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha, promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and mitofusin 2 expression, and deacetylates autophagy proteins promoting mitophagy[β][70]. Suboptimal health sirtuin 1 down reduces biogenesis, and intermittent fasting sirtuin 1 up promotes biogenesis and autophagy[β][62].

Calcium and calcineurin: calcium up activates calcineurin, dephosphorylates dynamin-related protein 1 serine 637, promoting fission[β][71]. Chronic stress calcium dysregulation via endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria tethering mitofusin 2 down may increase cytosolic calcium and calcineurin activity, driving fission. Calcium channel modulation therefore represents potential target.

Circadian rhythm: dynamin-related protein 1 and mitofusin 2 show circadian oscillation regulated by brain and muscle ARNT-like protein 1 and circadian locomotor output cycles kaput, with fission higher during active phase and fusion during rest phase[β][72]. Suboptimal health circadian disruption – flattened cortisol rhythm, delayed sleep phase, social jetlag – may disrupt dynamin-related protein 1 rhythm, contributing to morning-light evening-heavy pattern and explaining sleep and chronotherapy intervention mechanism via circadian-dynamin-related protein 1 rhythm restoration[β][73].

Butyrate (short-chain fatty acid) upregulates mitofusin 2 via histone deacetylase inhibition and G protein-coupled receptor 41/43, promoting fusion and gut barrier[β][74]. MitoQ and SS-31 (mitochondria-targeted antioxidants) reduce reactive oxygen species, mitochondrial DNA leakage, and cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation[β][75].

This regulator network provides molecular basis for time-window-target framework: early suboptimal health mild fission up adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase down calcium up mild – exercise, sleep/chronotherapy, butyrate; prolonged suboptimal health excessive fission mitophagy disorder mitochondrial DNA leakage cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes activation – intermittent fasting sirtuin 1-peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha, MitoQ/SS-31, combined.

**Critical appraisal**: Upstream regulator changes in SHSQ-25-defined suboptimal health are largely β homologous from exercise and circadian studies; direct measurement of adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase activity, sirtuin 1, calcium, and dynamin-related protein 1 circadian oscillation in suboptimal health requires future validation, which this review proposes as testable predictions.
